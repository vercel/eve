# Bluesky best practices

A sourced checklist of current tactics for writing on Bluesky. Use it as a pre-flight check, not a recipe: the house voice (first-person, plain, concrete) wins over any single rule.

## Voice & audience

- [ ] The audience skews technical, journalist/academic, and early-adopter. People are here on purpose to escape the noise, and they're allergic to marketing speak. Say the real thing.
- [ ] Be human, not corporate. Transparency, originality, and a conversational tone outperform polished promotion. Write like a person, not a brand account.
- [ ] Participation beats broadcasting. Replies and genuine questions often get more visibility than original posts, so a draft that invites a real reply earns its keep.

## Length & format

- [ ] Front-load the point. In a short feed, the first line decides whether anyone engages.
- [ ] One main idea per post. Write the full thought first, then cut to length: trimming forces clarity and kills filler.
- [ ] ~300 characters (graphemes, meaning visible characters, including emoji). It's a hard cap, but the tone can breathe more than an X post: wit and personality land here.
- [ ] Use threads (replies) for longer thoughts. Each post should still stand on its own; number them (1/4, 2/4) only if it genuinely helps readers track a long thread.

## Links

- [ ] No reach penalty. Bluesky does not downrank posts with external links, so link directly to the source instead of burying it or dropping it in a reply.
- [ ] Links are rich text (the AT Protocol stores them as "facets"), so the clickable metadata doesn't bloat the visible text. A link card preview can be attached separately.

## Media & accessibility

- [ ] Add alt text to every image. It's a community norm, not optional: posts without it get noticed, and alt text doesn't count against the 300-character post limit.
- [ ] Up to 4 images per post. Visual content (art, photography, screenshots) tends to perform well.
- [ ] Write alt text as a plain, useful description of what's in the image.

## Hashtags & discovery

- [ ] Hashtags are clickable and sortable, but use them sparingly: one or two topical tags at most, never a wall. Discovery leans on keywords in the post text more than on tags.
- [ ] Custom feeds are the real discovery engine: user-built topic channels, some keyed to specific hashtags or keywords. Posting with the right words surfaces you to people already watching that topic.
- [ ] Starter packs are curated account lists that newcomers follow in one click: a reason to keep a complete, recognizable profile.
