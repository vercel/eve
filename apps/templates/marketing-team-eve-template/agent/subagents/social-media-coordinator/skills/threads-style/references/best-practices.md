# Threads (Meta) best practices

A sourced checklist of current tactics for writing on Threads. Use it as a pre-flight check, not a recipe: the house voice (first-person, plain, concrete) wins over any single rule.

## Voice & audience

- [ ] Write like a person, not a brand. The platform's own framing is "Twitter's casual, meme-fluent cousin": funny, bold, low-polish content fits here in a way it doesn't on LinkedIn or a press release.
- [ ] Authenticity and vulnerability outperform polished, promotional copy. An unpolished, honest take resonates; a curated announcement does not.
- [ ] Treat Threads as a brand-awareness and conversation play, not a lead-gen or sales channel. Pitches and product demos land badly.
- [ ] Make it native. Don't repost an X/Twitter post verbatim: write for this audience, or use the post to *react to* and *extend* something you published elsewhere rather than mirror it.

## The hook

- [ ] Lead with something real: an observation, a small personal story, or a genuine question.
- [ ] Personal stories followed by a question are a consistently strong opener: they invite the reply that the algorithm rewards.
- [ ] No throat-clearing and no engagement bait. Skip "comment below 👇", "agree?", and "let that sink in": the algorithm rewards genuine value, not bait.

## Length & format

- [ ] One idea per post. The cap is 500 characters (see `post-specs.md`); you rarely need all of it. Short, skimmable posts with line breaks read best.
- [ ] Both short-form video (under ~60s) and plain text posts engage well: lead with whichever actually serves the idea, and don't add media just to add media.
- [ ] Polls are a strong, low-effort way to invite participation when a question genuinely splits.

## Topic tags

- [ ] One topic tag per post, maximum: that is the hard platform limit. There is no multi-tag strategy and no benefit to tag-stuffing.
- [ ] Tags can be a multi-word phrase (e.g. "Fitness Motivation"), not just a single word. Pick the one tag that best represents the post.
- [ ] Tags aid discovery, not guaranteed reach. Posts still surface in search whether or not a tag is added, so only tag when there's a genuinely relevant topic.

## Engagement & cadence

- [ ] Conversation is the whole game. Replying to comments on your own posts is the single highest-engagement-lift action measured on any platform (~42% per Buffer), so write posts that earn a reply and plan to answer them.
- [ ] Higher-effort actions weigh more in ranking: replies and profile clicks count for more than likes. Open-ended questions and prompts beat like-bait.
- [ ] The For You feed is algorithm-ranked and rewards lively, timely conversation; the Following feed is strictly reverse-chronological. Posting natively (not cross-posted) and engaging with a trend early helps reach.
- [ ] Scroll-past with no engagement is a negative signal: another reason to earn the reply, not just the impression.
- [ ] Consistency beats bursts: successful creators post ~1–3+ times/day, and replies on others' posts count toward that. (Cadence/timing is a scheduling note, not a drafting rule.)
