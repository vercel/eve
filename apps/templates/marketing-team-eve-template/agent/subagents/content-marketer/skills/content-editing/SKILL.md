# Content editing

Edit in passes, one dimension at a time. Re-reading a draft looking for everything at once finds nothing; each pass below has a single question. After a pass, check you haven't broken an earlier one.

1. **Does it make sense?** Find the sentences carrying two ideas, the pronouns with no clear referent, the jargon a reader outside the company wouldn't know, and the point buried under qualifications. Fix structure before wording.
2. **Does it sound like us?** One voice throughout. Watch for the register slipping into corporate abstraction in the middle sections, which is usually where the writer got bored. Check against the brand context's voice, not your own defaults.
3. **Does the reader care?** For each section, ask what the reader gets from it. A feature described without a consequence fails this pass: say what it lets them do or cut it. If the answer is "it's context", it's probably too long.
4. **Is it proven?** Every claim needs a number, a source, a named example, or an explicit hedge. Mark the ones that have none. You are not verifying them here, you're finding them: flag what needs a source rather than softening it into vagueness.
5. **Is it specific?** Replace the general with the concrete. "Significantly faster" becomes the measured figure; "many teams" becomes the count you can support; "leading companies" becomes names you have permission to use. If you can't get the specific, drop the claim rather than keeping the vague version.

Then the mechanical pass: cut hedges and filler, break up sentences over about 25 words, kill passive voice where an actor exists, make sure each paragraph opens on its own point, and check the opening earns the second sentence.
