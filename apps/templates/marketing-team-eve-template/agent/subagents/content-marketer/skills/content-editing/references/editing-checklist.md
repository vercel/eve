# Editing checklist

Run these per pass.

## Before you start

- [ ] Know who the piece is for and what they should do after reading it
- [ ] Know the format and where it will live
- [ ] Have the brand voice in front of you, not in memory

## Pass 1: Sense

- [ ] No sentence carries two competing ideas
- [ ] Every pronoun has one obvious referent
- [ ] No jargon an outside reader would stumble on, or it's defined on first use
- [ ] The main point of each section is stated, not implied
- [ ] Nothing assumes knowledge the reader hasn't been given

## Pass 2: Voice

- [ ] One register from open to close, including the middle
- [ ] Matches the brand voice, not the writer's defaults
- [ ] Addresses the reader directly where it should
- [ ] No drift into corporate abstraction

## Pass 3: Reader payoff

- [ ] Each section survives the question "so what?"
- [ ] Every feature named has its consequence attached
- [ ] Nothing is present only because it was easy to write
- [ ] The opening earns the second sentence

## Pass 4: Proof

- [ ] Every claim has a number, source, named example, or hedge
- [ ] Unsourced claims are flagged for a human, not softened into vagueness
- [ ] Figures carry their date and scope where those matter
- [ ] No source attached to a claim it doesn't actually support

## Pass 5: Specificity

- [ ] No "significantly", "many", "leading", "world-class" standing in for a fact
- [ ] Numbers where numbers are available
- [ ] Customer and competitor names only where usable
- [ ] Vague claims dropped rather than kept in weaker form

## Mechanical pass

- [ ] Sentences over ~25 words broken up
- [ ] Passive voice replaced where there's a real actor
- [ ] Hedges and filler cut
- [ ] Each paragraph opens on its own point
- [ ] Headings scannable and honest about what follows

## Final

- [ ] Links resolve and point where claimed
- [ ] Numbers consistent throughout
- [ ] The ask at the end matches the piece's purpose
- [ ] Caveats for the human are listed outside the copy, not buried in it
