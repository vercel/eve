# Common problems and fixes

Recurring failure patterns in marketing copy.

## Wall of features

**Looks like:** a list of capabilities, each described only as what it is.

**Fix:** attach the consequence to each one. "Role-based permissions" becomes "so a contractor can't see payroll". If a feature has no consequence you can name, it doesn't belong on the page.

## Corporate speak

**Looks like:** "leverage our robust platform to streamline operations". Abstraction with no image behind it.

**Fix:** say what actually happens, to whom. Replace the abstract noun with the verb it's hiding: "utilization of" becomes "using". The plain-English list in `writing-quality` covers the vocabulary.

## Weak opening

**Looks like:** context-setting, industry background, or a definition before the point.

**Fix:** cut until the first sentence carries the claim. Most drafts can lose their first paragraph entirely. The reader decides here whether to continue.

## Buried ask

**Looks like:** the next step appears once, in the last line, phrased as a suggestion.

**Fix:** one clear ask, placed where the reader is convinced rather than where the page ends. Say what happens after they click.

## No proof

**Looks like:** confident claims with nothing behind them. "Teams ship faster with us."

**Fix:** a number, a named customer, a screenshot, a mechanism. If none is available, say how it works instead of asserting the outcome. An explained mechanism beats an unbacked result.

## Generic claims

**Looks like:** sentences that would be true of any competitor. "Powerful, easy to use, built for scale."

**Fix:** the substitution test. Put a competitor's name in the sentence; if it still reads as true, the sentence says nothing. Replace it with what's true only of this product.

## Hedged into meaninglessness

**Looks like:** "may help some teams potentially reduce certain costs".

**Fix:** decide. Either the claim is supportable, in which case state it with its scope, or it isn't, in which case cut it. A precise narrow claim beats a broad mushy one.

## Undifferentiated middle

**Looks like:** a strong open and close with three interchangeable sections between.

**Fix:** usually a structure problem, not a wording one. Give each section a distinct job, or merge them. This is where a writer's attention lapses, so read the middle first on the voice pass.
