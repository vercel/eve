# Buyer-stage topics

What to write at each stage, and where to find the real questions.

## Unaware of the problem

They have the symptom, not the vocabulary. They aren't searching for your category.

- Write: the symptom named plainly, why it happens, what it costs. "Why our deploys keep breaking on Fridays", not "Why you need CI/CD".
- Find it in: support tickets, sales call objections, the "why is this happening" posts in community forums.
- Mistake to avoid: pitching. A reader who doesn't know they have a problem won't act on a solution.

## Comparing approaches

They know the problem and are weighing categories of fix, including doing nothing and building it themselves.

- Write: how the approaches genuinely differ on cost, effort, and failure mode. Include where yours is the wrong fit; that's what makes the rest credible.
- Find it in: "X vs Y" searches, Reddit and Hacker News threads where people argue about the approach, RFP questions.
- Mistake to avoid: a comparison rigged so your approach wins every row. Readers discount the whole page.

## Comparing products

They've picked an approach and are choosing a vendor. High intent, low patience.

- Write: specifics they can't get from a homepage. Pricing at their volume, migration path, real limits, what happens at scale, what the product doesn't do.
- Find it in: review sites, competitor changelogs and docs, the questions sales hears twice a week.
- Mistake to avoid: vague superiority claims. "More scalable" persuades nobody; a number does.

## Already sold

They've bought or are about to. The job is getting them to value fast.

- Write: setup, integration, the first useful outcome, common failure modes with fixes.
- Find it in: onboarding drop-off points, repeat support questions, the gap between docs and what people actually do first.
- Mistake to avoid: marketing tone. They want the steps.

## Mining questions honestly

Good sources for what people actually ask: search autocomplete and related searches, community threads in your space, review sites, your own support and sales logs, and competitor docs' FAQ sections.

Record the question in the words the person used. Rewriting it into marketing language loses the thing that made it findable.
